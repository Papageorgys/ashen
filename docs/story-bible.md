# Ashen Legacy — Story Bible

**Version 0.2 · Draft canon · 11 August 2026**
Canonical worldbuilding reference for the world of Aethyr. A dark-fantasy MMORPG.

> This is the single source of truth for the fiction. Website copy, quest text, cinematics, and item descriptions all derive from this — not the reverse. If the site and this bible disagree, the bible wins and the site gets corrected.

**Canon markers used below:**
- **[CANON]** — a locked fact. Do not contradict in player-facing text. Changing it requires a version bump + change-log note.
- **[OPEN]** — a deliberate ambiguity, unresolved on purpose. Do not quietly answer it in side content.
- **[GUIDANCE]** — craft/house-style direction.
- **Narrative owner: [ASSIGN]** — assign one canon owner before the team grows past two writers.

## 1 · Core Premise
Ashen Legacy is a dark-fantasy MMORPG on the continent of Aethyr, three centuries after the death of its immortal god-king plunged the world into ruin. Seven realms war over the remains of a broken empire in endless open-world siege warfare. No chosen hero, no ending — only territory, legacy, and whose name survives in the stone.

**Three pillars** (every story beat should serve at least one):
1. **Territory is identity.** Power is a castle everyone can see you holding, not a number on a sheet.
2. **The world is unforgiving, not cruel.** Harsh but fair; grim beauty, dark humour, hope worth fighting for. Never nihilistic.
3. **Legacy is the only victory.** You can't "beat" the game. The reward is being remembered. Write toward permanence.

**[TONE]** Grimdark with embers of hope. Ash-grey skies lit by firelight; ruined grandeur; defiant people. Never edgelord, never joyless. The world lost its god, not its will to live.

## 2 · Chronology
Years count from the Unmaking. Before = "Under the Crown"; after = "After Ash" (AA). Present day ≈ 84+ AA.

| Marker | Event | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Under the Crown | The Age of Sarethar | ~900 years of enforced peace under the immortal God-King. The land does not age. |
| Year 0 | The Unmaking | Sarethar dies; 900 years of borrowed life come due at once. Sky dims, Ash begins to fall. |
| Years 1–30 | The Sunless War | 30 years of siege, plague, ritual. Two-thirds of Aethyr depopulated. |
| ~Year 84 | The Seven Rise | Survivors form seven realms. The Long Truce is signed — and broken. |
| Present ("Season of Ash") | Open war | Truce dead; castles change hands weekly. The player enters here. |

**[OPEN] Q3 — exact present-day year.** Kept soft so live seasons can advance in-world time without contradiction. Use relative references ("three generations since the war"), never absolute, until the narrative owner sets a calendar.

## 3 · The God-King and the Unmaking
For ~900 years Aethyr knew one ruler: **Sarethar, the Sunless Crown** — god made king or king made god, unsettled and now moot. Under him the land did not age, wounds closed, harvests never failed.

**[CANON]** Sarethar's immortality and his age's prosperity were **not gifts** — they were drawn slowly and invisibly from the land and every living thing on it. Aethyr flourished because its god was **feeding**. Never soften this into a benevolent golden age.

On the night of the **Unmaking**, Sarethar died and 900 years of borrowed life came due in a moment: sky guttered, harvests failed, first Ash fell.

**[OPEN] Q1 — who killed Sarethar?** The central mystery; must stay open. Every realm tells a self-flattering version (jealous archmage, betrayed lover, his own hand, an outside power). Canonise none. This is the setting's most valuable unspent story asset.

> "He did not fall like a man. He came apart like a promise, and the whole world felt the debt." — *The First Lament*, author unknown

## 4 · The Nature of Ash (Magic System)
Sarethar's borrowed life burned out of the world as **Ash** — a fine grey fall that never settles, worked into everything. Every school of magic is a way of touching a dead god's remains.

**[CANON] THE ONE LAW: Ash always collects.** Power from a dead god is never free — it costs heat, years, memory, or life. Governs every caster, item, and faction without exception. It's why the strongest mages are rarely the oldest. No "free" magic, ever.

| Discipline | Effect | Cost | Legality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Embercraft** | Coaxes remembered heat of Ash into flame; common battle-magic | Burns out the caster's body over time | Legal everywhere |
| **The Hollow Arts** | Borrows the debt itself — years, memories, the dead | Paid in life and self | Worshipped by the Covenant; hanging offence elsewhere |
| **Runewarding** | Binds Ash into stone, steel, oath — wards, arms, sigils | Slow, exacting, expensive | Legal and essential everywhere |

**[GUIDANCE]** When inventing any spell/item/ability, **name its cost before its effect.** If you can't say what the Ash collects in return, it isn't finished.

## 5 · The Long Night
Periodically the upper-air Ash thickens and swallows the sun: the **Long Night**. Not weather — the world re-living the moment its god died, and in that memory things wake. Light-fearing creatures pour out; the dead grow restless; at the deepest hearts, a figure in a pale crown walks.

**[CANON]** A **recurring world event**, not a one-time apocalypse. Warring realms call bitter temporary truces to survive it. It has ended more alliances than any siege. Mechanically it's the pressure-release valve against faction stalemate; treat it as a force of nature that briefly outranks politics.

**[OPEN] Q2 — what the Pale Sovereign is** and its true link to Sarethar is reserved (fragment of the god? its killer? the debt made flesh? something the Unmaking simply let in?). Foreshadow freely; explain nothing.

## 9 · Legends of the Age
Named legends are scarce **on purpose** — the player is the protagonist of their own legacy.
- **Vareth the Unbroken (The Founder)** — held one hill through the war's worst year; the castle bears his name; Ember Court claims his blood, three realms dispute it.
- **Ysold of the Hollow (The Traitor)** — Sarethar's high priest; first to practise the Hollow arts openly; saint to the Covenant, criminal to six realms.
- **Ser Callum Grey (The Warden)** — founded the Pale Wardens; faced the Pale Sovereign at the first Long Night and lived; watchtowers burn a grey lantern for him.
- **You (The Unwritten)** — the Monument has empty stone; whether your name is carved there is the only unwritten part of this history.

## 11 · Naming & Style Guide
**Always-capitalised canon terms:** Ash (the substance), the Unmaking, the Sunless War, the Long Night, the Long Truce, the Season of Ash, the God-King, the Sunless Crown, After Ash (AA). Generic uses ("ash from a fire") stay lowercase.

- **British spelling** (smouldering, honour, defence) is house standard; don't mix with US spelling on one surface.
- **Naming sound:** places/realms lean hard Germanic/Norse and clipped (Vareth, Aethyr, Dwar, Ysold); magic/titles lean elemental-compound (Emberweaver, Runewarding, Ashblade). Keep new names in these two families.
- **Voice:** terse, weighty, a little archaic. Short declaratives ("Ash always collects.").

**[GUIDANCE] Do-not list:** no modern slang; no puns in place/realm names; no "chosen one" framing; no confirmed answer to Q1 (who killed Sarethar) or Q2 (the Pale Sovereign); no benevolent-golden-age nostalgia for Sarethar. Any of these in player-facing copy is a canon bug.

## 14 · Party, Fellowship & Companions
*Who — and what — may stand under one banner. The rules of fellowship are fiction and system at once; they must agree (§10). Every rule below either grounds a shipped mechanic or names a cost (§4, The One Law).*

### 14.1 The Warband — five souls under one banner
**[CANON] A warband is up to five souls.** Five is not an arbitrary limit — it is the largest band that can move through Ash-choked country without a stumble in the dark going unseen. Grounds the shipped `MAX_PARTY_SIZE = 5`.

- Every warband flies one **banner**, borne by a **banner-bearer** (the party leader). The banner is the thing rival realms see standing on the map — *territory is identity* (Pillar 1). Cut down the bearer and the band scatters; the banner is the band.
- Larger hosts exist, but they are **war-bands of banners** — several warbands mustered under one horn for a siege, or to survive a Long Night. A muster is summoned, never standing. (Grounds the shared **muster** against a world boss; see §14.7 and §15.5.)

### 14.2 Who may stand together — souls
Aethyr is too thinned of people to be choosy. The war made strange companions; a band of five is five of whoever is still breathing.

- **Any race, any calling.** Cross-race and cross-class warbands are the norm, not the exception. No lineage refuses another at the fire when the dark comes.
- **Cross-realm is the hard line.** The realms are at war (§6). Two souls sworn to warring realms **cannot share a banner on contested ground.** Three lawful exceptions, each already a story hook:
  1. **The Long Night truce.** While the sun is swallowed (§5), *all* banners may stand together; every oath is suspended until the grey morning. This is why the Long Night has ended more alliances than any siege — bands forged in the dark rarely survive the dawn that dissolves them.
  2. **Neutral ground.** The Founders' Monument and every Gilded Compact hold are truce-bound by old contract. Blades stay sheathed; a band may form across realm lines within the walls, and dissolves at the gate.
  3. **A Compact Contract.** The **Gilded Compact** will broker a mercenary bond across enemy lines — for a price. *"Every crown has a price."* The bond holds exactly as long as the coin does; when the ledger closes, so does the truce, mid-field if need be. **[GUIDANCE]** Name the cost: a Compact-bonded blade is trusted by neither realm and owned by a third.
- **The Free Holds swear to no crown** (§6) and so take anyone. A Free Hold banner is the one standing band that may hold souls of any origin without a contract — the neutral meeting-blood of Aethyr, and the natural home of player-driven guild fiction (Pillar-agnostic, *player-driven*).

### 14.3 What may stand with you — the beast, the bound, the warded
A banner's fifth place need not be a living soul. Each of the three below **counts as one soul in the five** — flesh or ash, five is five. And each collects (§4).

- **The tamed beast** *(Beastcaller / Ferran Beastkin).* A packmate takes a place in the band and fights as a soul. **Cost:** the bond feeds on the tamer's own Ash-warmth to stay bound; a starved bond turns feral and must be put down. The rarest bond of all is a **saddled Ash-Drake** (§8) — server-recognised, and only one may fly under a realm at a time.
- **The bound dead** *(Hollow-Touched / the Hollow Covenant).* A **Cinderwight** (§8) may be bound to a banner as a servant-at-arms. **Cost:** it burns the binder's *years* — carry the dead too long and you age into the thing you carry. Legal only beneath a Covenant banner; a hanging offence under six realms (§4). A band that marches with the bound dead **cannot pass the gate of a lawful hold** without hiding it — a standing constraint, not a flavour note.
- **The warded construct** *(Runepriest / Runewarding).* A graven stone sentinel stands as a soul. **Cost:** its sigils must be re-cut after every field; a construct left to outlast its wards forgets whose side it was graven for and turns on the band.

**[CANON] No band of six.** A beast, a bound dead, or a construct **fills** a soul's place — it does not add one. There is no warband of six by counting a hound. This keeps every companion honest against `MAX_PARTY_SIZE = 5`.

### 14.4 Bonds — the Ember-bond
Grounds the shipped affinity system (`BOND_AT = 6`), the shield-bond, and the **marks** a soul carries.

- **Souls who survive six fields together forge an Ember-bond.** Bonded pairs fight better beside one another — the bond is real remembered warmth, and it shows on the field.
- **The One Law taxes even friendship.** A bonded pair **shares the burn.** When one is wounded to a hair's breadth, the other feels it and falters (the *wounded* mark). When one falls, the survivor carries the grief home (the *survivor* mark, bound to grief). You do not get to love in Aethyr for free.
- **Marks are canon-legible biography** — the world writes on a soul, newest first. The set the systems keep: **oath** (the day they swore in — the player's own authorship, Pillar 3), **bond**, **clutch** (last one standing), **survivor**, **wounded**, **valor** (carried the fight), **slayer** (first of the clan to fell a kind of beast), **ascend** (came into their strength), **triumph** (a clan-defining victory). A name earns its place in the stone one mark at a time.

### 14.5 The dawn horn — showing up is the war
Grounds the daily rally streak and its seven-day feast.

- A band that answers the horn at **dawn**, day after day, builds a **streak**. Every **seventh dawn is a feast** — the war rewards the band that keeps standing, not the band that was chosen. *There is no chosen one; there is who mustered this morning.* (Pillar 3, and the daily-grind heart of the game.)

### 14.6 The march — distance is danger
Grounds banner travel time across the map.

- Banners take **real time** to cross country. The Ash does not care that you are in a hurry; a far castle is a far castle, and the road between is where bands are lost. Where your banner *is* — not only where it fights — is part of holding ground (Pillar 1).

### 14.7 What a band is for
- You cannot win Aethyr; you can only be remembered in it (Pillar 3). A warband's purpose is not victory — it is to be **the five names carved together** when the Founders' Monument is read aloud. The Monument keeps room for banners, not only for heroes (§7, §9).

## 15 · Quests, Contracts & Stories
*How we write the world's work so it respects the lore. This is a daily-grind MMORPG (§1) — most content is repeatable Contracts, not one-shot arcs.*

### 15.1 The rules every quest obeys
1. **Serve a pillar** (territory / unforgiving-not-cruel / legacy). If it serves none, cut it.
2. **Name the cost before the reward** (§4, The One Law). A quest that gives without taking is a canon bug.
3. **Never "the chosen one."** The player is *a* soul, not *the* soul. No prophecy names them.
4. **Keep Q1 and Q2 open.** Foreshadow who killed Sarethar and what the Pale Sovereign is — never answer. Give players the *question* as treasure.
5. **House style holds** (§11): British spelling, terse and a little archaic, no modern slang, no golden-age nostalgia for Sarethar.
6. **Grim, not cruel** (Pillar 2). Loss is fair and earns something remembered; never loss for its own sake.

### 15.2 Contracts — the repeatable spine (daily / weekly / monthly)
Frame Contracts as work the war never runs out of. Each names a cost. (Feeds the shipped Contracts board.)

| Contract | Cadence | The work | The cost it collects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ash-Watch** | Daily | Stand a dawn-watch over a buried village in the Ashen Reach; Cinderwights rise at first light and must be put back down. | A watched night gives no rest — a soul who watches gains no recovery that dawn. |
| **The Ledger's Errand** | Daily (Gilded Compact) | Carry a sealed debt across a contested road and deliver it unopened. | You may not learn what you carry; refusing to read the seal is the job. |
| **Tithe of Ash** | Weekly (any realm) | Gather fallen Ash to feed a realm's runewards. | Gathered Ash collects on the gatherer — a week of tithing greys the skin. |
| **The Truce Table** | Monthly | Broker one cross-realm Compact Contract (§14.2) and see it held to term. | A broker is trusted by neither side, and remembered by both. |

**[GUIDANCE]** New Contracts follow the same shape: one line of work, one named cost, a reward in coin **and** in warmth (rally, bonds, marks). Never coin alone.

### 15.3 Starting arc — *"The Ashen Reach"* (newcomer questline)
Set in the starting region (§7). Teaches the One Law and the no-chosen-one truth by feel, not lecture. Five beats:

1. **Swearing in.** You take the oath — your first *oath* mark, the one piece of the history you author yourself (§14.4). An old serjeant tells you plainly: *nobody remembers the swearing. They remember the standing.*
2. **The buried village.** Dig out a hamlet the Ash took. Inside, a **Cinderwight** still labours to finish a spell it began ninety years dead (§8). You end it — or you leave it. It was never cruel. Only unfinished.
3. **First blood, first cost.** Your first Embercraft. The quest makes you feel the burn it takes back (§4) — a small scar you keep. Power announced its price on your first day.
4. **The horn at dawn.** Ride to your first dawn rally (§14.5). You learn the war is not won by the chosen. It is won by who shows up again tomorrow.
5. **The empty stone.** Stand at the Founders' Monument (§7). Your name is not on it. Nobody promises it will be. That blank space is the whole game (Pillar 3).

### 15.4 Realm arcs — one seed each
Keep every realm morally three-dimensional (§6 faction notes). One hook apiece:

- **Ember Court — *"The Unbroken Line."*** Prove Vareth's blood to seat a claim on his castle. The twist: the proof is a lie everyone needs to be true. (Their certainty is their flaw — do not let them be simple heroes.)
- **Hollow Covenant — *"The Dead Kept Faith."*** Learn a Hollow rite to speak with a fallen comrade one last time. **Cost:** years of your own life, paid in advance, visible on your face after. (Devotion, not villainy.)
- **Free Holds — *"No Leash."*** Not a plot — a bounty board, a bar-room brawl, and a road out. Leave the story to the band. (Light-touch by design.)
- **Verdant Reclamation — *"The Ash Will Feed the Roots."*** Let a ruined keep fall to the forest instead of retaking it. **Cost:** a castle of real strategic worth, willingly given up. (Ideological, not naive.)
- **Gilded Compact — *"Every Crown a Price."*** Foreclose on a realm's war-debt, then watch the siege your ledger set in motion. (The quiet antagonist of every economic storyline.)
- **Pale Wardens — *"Hold the Door."*** Man a lonely watchtower through one Long Night. The single arc where a player *sees* the real threat plainly — and cannot make anyone believe it. (The foreshadowing voice; light a grey lantern for Ser Callum Grey, §9.)
- **Sunless Choir — *"He Is Only Quiet."*** A conversion arc in which you never learn whether the Choir is ten thousand strong or ten. (Q6 — the uncertainty is the horror.)

### 15.5 The Long Night — the world-event chain
The recurring event that briefly outranks politics (§5). Server-wide, and the natural home of the shared **muster** (§14.1) against the **Pale Sovereign** (§8). Four beats:

1. **The Last Dawn.** The upper Ash thickens; the horns of every realm sound the same warning for once.
2. **The Truce Table.** Warring banners are forced to stand together (§14.2) — old enemies in one war-band, oaths suspended till morning.
3. **The Standing.** Hold ground through the dark against Gloomstalkers and the restless dead. At the deepest hour the Pale Sovereign walks and rearranges the field around it. **Never answers Q2.**
4. **The Grey Morning.** Count who is left. Carve the Monument with the Long Night's dead and its server-first deeds (§7). Then the truce dissolves — and the bands forged in the dark must decide what they are by daylight.

### 15.6 Foreshadowing the reserved mysteries
How to spend Q1 and Q2 without ever cashing them:
- **Q1 (who killed Sarethar):** seed seven contradictory records across the realms, each version true *to the realm that keeps it* and flattering to it (§3). A player who reads all seven holds seven truths and no answer.
- **Q2 (the Pale Sovereign):** a Warden who looked on its face at the first Long Night and lived (§9) — and still cannot say what he saw. The gap where his description should be is the treasure.

## 16 · Canon Reconciliation — game data vs. bible
The bible is the source of truth (masthead). The shipped game predates this document; where they disagree, **the site gets corrected** — but flag the drift here first, never silently rewrite. Narrative owner [ASSIGN] to resolve.

**Already in agreement (fiction now matches system):**
- **Warband size = 5.** Canon adopts the shipped `MAX_PARTY_SIZE = 5` as the "warband of five" (§14.1). ✔
- **Ember-bond at six fields.** Canon adopts `BOND_AT = 6` (§14.4). ✔
- **Marks, rally streak + seventh-day feast, banner travel, shared muster** — all shipped systems, all now given canon fiction in §14–§15. ✔

**Open drift to resolve:**
- **Race names.** The game currently ships **six** playable races (Hyunan, Sylvani, Nocturi, Grakhar, Durgan, Kaemari) whose passives already echo the canon archetypes of §10 — e.g. Nocturi *"Dark Bargain"* ≈ the Hollow Arts; Durgan *"Forge Blood"* ≈ Highland Dwar (siege/craft); Sylvani *"Grove Sight"* ≈ Sylvan Elf (scout); Grakhar *"Ash Hide"* ≈ Emberkin (ash/burn); Hyunan *"River Discipline"* ≈ Ashborn Human; Kaemari *"Soulbind"* ≈ Ferran Beastkin/binder. Decide: are these the **realm-tongue names** of the §10 archetypes, or do they get renamed to §10 canon? Either way, **Q4's seventh race stays the named blank** until designed here.
- **Realm names on the War Table atlas.** The shipped atlas labels seven realms (Sylvani, Ravenfold, Durgan, Hyunan, Kaemari, Grakhar, Nocturi) that predate the canonical seven of §6 (Ember Court, Hollow Covenant, Free Holds, Verdant Reclamation, Gilded Compact, Pale Wardens, Sunless Choir). *(The seventh atlas realm, "Ravenfold," was invented only to reach a count of seven — exactly the "said seven, showed other" drift §6 exists to stop.)* Reconcile the atlas labels to §6 before more surfaces reference them.
- **Class names.** Shipped classes (Bulwark Knight, Twin Edge Duelist, Windshot Ranger, Runecaster, Lightbearer, …) predate the §10 role/class table (Ashblade, Ironward, Emberweaver, Oathkeeper, …). Map old → new, or fold the shipped names in as awakenings, before class names reach new player-facing copy.
